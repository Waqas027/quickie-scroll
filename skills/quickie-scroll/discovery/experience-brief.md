# The experience brief — turn a subject into a real website

*Run at the close of the interview, before chapters or post-film sections are chosen.*

**Read with:** [`interview.md`](interview.md) · [`sections/post-film.md`](../sections/post-film.md) ·
[`assets/sourcing.md`](../assets/sourcing.md)

The subject is not a section list. A premium website has an emotional entry point *and* a
useful path to the decision it is meant to support. Write this compact brief in `README.md`
before designing the film or the page:

```text
VISITOR          Who arrives, what they already know, and their intent.
PRIMARY ACTION   One verb: book, buy, compare, configure, enquire, discover, subscribe.
PROMISE          The specific benefit or feeling the experience establishes.
DECISION INPUTS  The information a visitor needs before acting.
CONTENT MODEL    The real entities, categories and media needed for those inputs.
INTERACTIONS     The one or two actions that make the decision easier.
TRUST            What can be verified; what must be labelled demo or omitted.
ASSET PLAN       Supplied / generated / web-sourced assets and any rights constraints.
```

Do not invent a full product catalogue, live availability, prices, awards, reviews,
destinations or businesses. Ask only for the missing decision that would materially alter
the build. Otherwise use a clearly-labelled demo data set or choose an interaction that
does not need fictitious facts.

## Domain patterns — choose the job, not the template

These are quality checks, not mandatory section recipes:

| Subject | A credible visitor journey usually makes room for |
|---|---|
| Travel | destination discovery with real imagery, seasonal/experience cues, an itinerary or trip-preference interaction, stay/route detail when verified, then a booking/enquiry handoff |
| Shop / product | collection discovery, product detail and variants, materials/benefits, comparison or recommendation where useful, then a basket or purchase handoff |
| Food / restaurant | signature dishes, menu/order path, provenance or preparation, location/hours only when real, and a reservation/order action |
| Hospitality | room/property atmosphere, accommodation options, local experiences, amenity/arrival details, then availability or enquiry—not invented booking inventory |
| Service / SaaS | the workflow, outcomes, credible proof, integration or use-case exploration, then demo/contact/signup |

The burger and travel references are a useful visual bar: full-bleed imagery, editorial
composition, a clear hierarchy, and connected storytelling. They are not permission to
copy their branding, layout, text, claims or fictitious testimonials.

## UI/UX Pro Max pass

Before implementation, invoke **UI/UX Pro Max** if that skill is installed and available.
Use its guidance to select type scale, color contrast, responsive layout, interaction
states, keyboard/focus behavior and component composition. Preserve this skill's film,
asset and continuity laws when advice conflicts. If UI/UX Pro Max is unavailable, apply
the same checks directly; do not halt a build or claim it was used.

The resulting UI must support the primary action on touch, keyboard and reduced-motion
paths. A beautiful scroll effect that hides the booking, purchase or exploration task has
failed the experience brief.
